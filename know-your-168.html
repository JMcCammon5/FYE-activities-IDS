Step 3

Sort it out: the Covey matrix

Six things landed on your plate this week. Sort each one into a quadrant. There's no single right answer — groups almost always disagree, and that's normal. Pick where you would put it.

First, here's the model you're using

Time management isn't really about your calendar. It's about knowing what to prioritize when everything feels urgent. The matrix below is one way to practice that instinct on paper, before you need it under pressure.

Everything on your plate gets sized up by two questions. Is it urgent — does it have a clock running on it right now? And is it important — does it actually move you toward something that matters? Those two questions make four boxes.

Q1 · Crisis

Urgent + important

It's on fire and it matters. You handle this now, no debate.

Q2 · Goals

Important, not urgent

Matters, but nothing forces you to it today. Studying ahead, sleep, relationships, planning. This is where you actually get ahead.

Q3 · Interruptions

Urgent, not important

Feels pressing because someone else made it your problem. Doesn't move you forward.

Q4 · Distraction

Neither urgent nor important

Pure noise. Feels like nothing, costs you real hours.

Most people, not just students, live in Q1 and Q4: putting out fires, then collapsing into distraction to recover from the fire. Q2 is where progress actually happens, and it's the one quadrant nothing ever drags you to. You have to choose it on purpose.
